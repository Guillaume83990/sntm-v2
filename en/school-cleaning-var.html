School cleaning in the Var

A school is not cleaned whenever you like, but whenever you can. That is the whole trade.

01The three seasons of the year 02Six areas, six constraints 03Touch points 04Frequently asked questions

In a school, the floor area is never the problem. The window always is.

A shop can push its visit back by an hour. A school cannot. Everything happens between the last pupil out in the evening and the first one in next morning, in rooms that several hundred people will occupy from the first bell.

That is what makes the school calendar structural: it dictates not only when you work but what you can do. Deep resets and floor treatments have only two windows in the year.

01 — The calendar

Three seasons, three ways of working

01

During term time

The daily rhythm

Classrooms, corridors and washrooms are reset every day, outside pupil hours. The constraint is not the floor area but the window: everything must be done between the evening exit and the morning arrival, without exception and with no chance to catch up.

02

At the short holidays

The deep reset

A week of closure allows what term time never does: lifting the marks embedded in the circulation areas, reworking the skirting and the corners, treating the zones that daily upkeep merely holds steady.

03

The summer

The heavy work

It is the only window in the year for work on the floors: stripping, restoration, sealing. A school floor takes ten months of intensive footfall; if it is not reworked then, it degrades from one year to the next with no way back.

In a school, the calendar does not just say when you work. It says what you are allowed to do.

02 — The areas

Six areas, six constraints

A school is not a uniform surface. Each area has its own rhythm, its own difficulty and its own standard.

01

Classrooms

Floors, desks, touch points and bins. The difficulty is not the cleaning itself but the reset: everything back in its place before the next morning.

02

Circulation areas

Corridors, staircases, handrails and landings. These are the hardest-worked surfaces in a school, and where the floor wears fastest.

03

Washrooms

The most sensitive and most scrutinised item. It needs a high frequency and consistent restocking, not just a visit.

04

The canteen

Greasy floors, tables, the areas around the serving points. A daily reset after each sitting, in a very short window.

05

Staff rooms and offices

Workspaces to be treated as offices, with the same touch points and the same discretion around paperwork.

06

The immediate surroundings

Entrance, covered play area, steps and railings. This is the first image of the school, for pupils and families alike.

03 — Hygiene

What three hundred hands touch every day

In a school, hygiene is not decided on the large surfaces but on a dozen small points that everyone touches, several times a day, without thinking.

They are what needs a high frequency and a suitable product, not the square metres of corridor. They appear explicitly in the contract scope.

The floors

Ten months of footfall, one window to put it right

A school floor takes footfall no other building sees: hundreds of crossings a day, concentrated into a few minutes at every bell, for ten months. The circulation floor wears first, always in the same places.

Daily upkeep holds it steady, it does not repair it. The reset is scheduled for the holidays, and that is the only possible moment in the year.

04 — Frequently asked questions

What head teachers ask

Do you work during lesson time?

In a school, visits are timed after the pupils leave or before they arrive. It is an organisational constraint, and it is also the only way to work properly in occupied premises. The exact slot is agreed with the head.

How are the school holidays handled?

They are not quiet periods, they are the working windows. The short holidays are for deep resets of the circulation areas, the summer for heavy work on the floors. The calendar follows the education authority's, not ours.

How often for the washrooms?

Higher than for anything else, and it is the item a school contract is judged on. The exact frequency depends on the number of pupils and the number of washroom blocks: it is set after a visit and written into the contract.

Are the products suitable for a building open to the public?

In a building open to the public, the choice of products, their dilution and their storage fall within a defined framework. These points are settled with the head before work starts, and appear in the contract.

Do you also treat the floors in depth?

Yes, and it is often what is most lacking in a school. A school floor takes ten months of intensive footfall; without an annual reset it degrades year on year. Heavy work is scheduled for the summer.

Which towns do you cover?

Saint-Tropez and the whole Gulf — Ramatuelle, Gassin, Grimaud, Cogolin, Sainte-Maxime, Cavalaire-sur-Mer, La Croix-Valmer — plus other towns in the Var depending on the contract.

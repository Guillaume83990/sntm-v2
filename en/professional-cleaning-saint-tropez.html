Professional cleaning in Saint-Tropez

What a maintenance contract actually covers, area by area, and what it should guarantee you.

01What is covered 02By type of premises 03What to insist on 04Frequently asked questions

Premises are not judged on the day of the visit. They are judged three months later, when you look at the grout, the corners and the thresholds.

That is where the difference shows between a cleaning job and a maintenance contract. The first deals with what can be seen. The second preserves the condition of the premises, which costs considerably less than a full restoration every two years.

01 — The scope

What is covered, area by area

Most disputes over a maintenance contract come from a vague scope. Here are the areas of a commercial property and what each one needs. The final rhythm is set after the visit, based on your actual footfall.

03 — The contract

Five things to demand of any contractor

Including of us. A maintenance contract without these five points will end in disagreement, however sound the company.

  1. A written scope

    Area by area, with what is included and what is not. A single line reading “cleaning of premises” always ends in disagreement.

  2. The floor material identified

    Marble, travertine, terracotta, polished concrete and porcelain do not call for the same products. A contractor who never asks will damage your floors while cleaning them.

  3. A frequency that varies across the year

    On the Gulf, a single rhythm across twelve months is a badly designed contract. August has nothing in common with February.

  4. A named contact

    A person to call, not a switchboard. And a regular review to adjust whatever needs adjusting.

  5. A quote drawn up after a visit

    Two properties of the same size can require very different hours. A price given over the phone will be revised, so it is not a price.

04 — Frequently asked questions

What people ask us before signing

Regular contract or one-off job?

Both. A regular contract suits premises open to the public, where condition deteriorates quickly without follow-up. A one-off job answers a dated need: a restoration, the end of a building site, a floor reset, a change of season.

Do you work outside opening hours?

Yes, that is the most common arrangement. The slot is agreed when the contract is signed and appears in the written scope, alongside the areas and the frequency.

How is the quote drawn up?

After an on-site visit. Square metres say nothing about the actual work: the floor material, the ceiling height, the number of washrooms and the footfall weigh far more than the area.

Does the season change the service?

Considerably. Between June and September, a business on the Gulf takes footfall on a scale unmatched by the rest of the year, with sand and salt wearing the floors. A varied frequency beats a single rhythm across twelve months.

What sets you apart from an ordinary cleaning company?

Floor treatment. Many maintenance companies clean a floor without knowing what it is made of, and end up damaging it. Identifying marble, travertine or an encaustic tile and matching the product changes the immediate result, and the lifespan of the floor.

Can the contract change during the year?

Yes, and it should. An extra room, a change in footfall or a new scheduling constraint all justify an adjustment. That is what the regular review in the contract is for.
